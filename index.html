What is this tool?

Medications come with multiple identifiers: RXCUI for clinical concepts, NDC for packaging, ATC for therapeutic classification. Translating between them sounds simple but hides a real problem. A single ingredient like fluticasone is sold as a nasal spray, a skin cream, and an asthma inhaler, each with a different correct ATC code. Standard mapping tools return all three, polluting your data with wrong-route codes. MedCode Lookup fixes that by validating the route before choosing.

A concrete example: what is fluticasone?

Fluticasone is a corticosteroid, a potent anti-inflammatory drug. The exact same molecule is formulated three different ways for three different conditions, and each formulation gets its own ATC code:

  • Nasal spray for allergic rhinitis (e.g., Flonase) R01AD08
  • Skin cream / ointment for eczema and inflammatory skin conditions (e.g., Cutivate) D07AC17
  • Inhaler for asthma maintenance (e.g., Flovent, ingredient in Advair) R03BA05

The right ATC depends on which product you're looking at, not just the ingredient. That's the gap MedCode Lookup closes.

Standard ingredient-level mapping

Input

RXCUI 41126 IN

fluticasone, the ingredient, no formulation specified

  • R01AD08 fluticasone (nasal preparation)
  • R03BA05 fluticasone (inhaled glucocorticoid for asthma)

What do these codes mean?

Four code systems show up across pharmacy informatics. Each one answers a different question.

RXCUI 🆔

RxNorm Concept Unique Identifier

Identifies: a specific clinical drug concept.

Example

1797907

fluticasone propionate 0.05 MG/ACTUAT Metered Dose Nasal Spray

Maintained by U.S. National Library of Medicine · updated monthly

RxNorm normalizes the many ways a drug can be named (generic, branded, abbreviated) into one ID.

ATC 🧬

Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical

Identifies: a drug's therapeutic and chemical classification.

Example: R01AD08
R Respiratory system
01 Nasal preparations
A Decongestants & other nasal prep.
D Corticosteroids
08 Fluticasone

Maintained by WHO Collaborating Centre for Drug Statistics Methodology · updated annually

ATC organizes drugs by where they act and how they work, making class-level analysis possible.

NDC 📦

National Drug Code

Identifies: a specific drug packaging from a specific labeler.

Example: 00378-4517-93
00378 – 4517 – 93

Labeler · Product · Package

Maintained by U.S. FDA · updated continuously

Every bottle, tube, or vial on a U.S. pharmacy shelf has an NDC. Same drug, different package → different NDC.

DFG 💊

Dose Form Group

Identifies: how a drug is administered (route + form category).

Example values
  • Nasal Product → nasal
  • Ophthalmic Product → ophthalmic
  • Topical Product → topical
  • Oral Product → oral

Maintained by U.S. National Library of Medicine · part of RxNorm

DFG is the key to route-aware mapping. "Nasal Product" tells us this fluticasone goes in the nose, not on skin or in lungs.

How does it work?

The route-aware resolver runs three steps. Each step has a specific question it's trying to answer.

Where the data comes from

All clinical drug data comes from RxNav, a free public API run by the U.S. National Library of Medicine. We do not store or transmit any patient information.

How the route filter works

Every administration route has a small set of valid ATC anatomical groups. Nasal products only belong in R01 (nasal preparations). Eye drops only belong in S01 (ophthalmologicals). The filter compares each candidate ATC to the route's allowed groups.

What we keep, what we reject

A code is kept when its anatomical group matches the drug's route. A code is rejected when the same ingredient has been registered under a different formulation. Both are explained on the result page so you can verify the logic yourself.

Who is this for?

  • Clinical informaticists validating medication mappings
  • EHR and HIE data engineers building drug indexes
  • Pharmacy and quality measure teams curating drug lists
  • Clinical product teams authoring drug-class rules

Not for direct patient care decisions. Always verify against the authoritative source (WHO ATC, RxNorm, FDA NDC) before production use.

Limitations

Combination products

Drugs with multiple active ingredients may produce multiple ATC codes, one per component. This is expected behavior.

Coverage gaps

NLM's ATCPROD curated mapping covers ~97% of common drugs. Newer or rare formulations may rely on our route-filter fallback, which is heuristic-based.

Multi-route ingredients

Some ingredients are legitimately classified in multiple ATC anatomical groups. When you look up an ingredient (TTY=IN), the tool returns all valid mappings without filtering.

Not a clinical decision support system

This is a data validation and exploration tool. Always verify results against authoritative sources before clinical use.

MedCode Lookup is a validation and exploration tool, not a clinical decision support system. Results are derived from public RxNorm data and a curated route-classification heuristic. The mapping logic may produce false positives or false negatives, particularly for combination products, ingredient-level concepts, and drugs with legitimate cross-route ATC assignments. Always verify results against authoritative sources (WHO ATC, RxNorm, FDA NDC Directory) before use in production systems. Not for use in patient care decisions.
